This model was trained using our own original image dataset. It predicts whether a facial expression is closer to happy or sad based on the examples we provided in Teachable Machine.

Project Overview

For this project, we used Google Teachable Machine to train an image classification model using original photos contributed by both group members. We organized our dataset into two classes: Happy and Sad.

Our goal was to explore how machine learning models recognize patterns in facial expressions, while also thinking critically about bias, training data, and the limits of AI systems.

Dataset Summary

  • 2 classes: Happy and Sad
  • 100 original images contributed by each group member
  • Images combined by class, not by person
  • Variation in angle, lighting, background, and facial expression

How to Use It

  1. Click the Start Model button.

  3. Try different facial expressions in front of the webcam.

Why This Matters

This project connects to ideas from Unmasking AI by Joy Buolamwini in a really clear way. We realized that a model is only as good as the data it's trained on. If the dataset is limited, messy, or biased, the model can pick up patterns that don't actually work well in real situations. Even something simple like a facial expression classifier reflects the choices we made when collecting and labeling our data.

Building this also showed us that AI isn't neutral. How accurate it is depends a lot on who is included in the dataset, how we define categories like “happy” or “sad,” and what kinds of patterns the model ends up focusing on.
